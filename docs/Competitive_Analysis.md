# EventRadius.ai Competitive Analysis

## Executive Summary

EventRadius.ai competes in the event management and discovery space with three main categories of competitors:
1. **Ticketing Platforms** (Eventbrite) - Focus on ticket sales
2. **Community Platforms** (Meetup, Nextdoor) - Focus on community building
3. **Social Platforms** (Facebook Events) - Focus on social sharing

**Our Unique Position**: AI-powered dynamic pricing + proactive notifications for perishable inventory (cinema seats, fitness classes, restaurant tables).

---

## 🏆 Main Competitors

### 1. Eventbrite (The Ticketing Giant)

**Founded**: 2006
**Market Position**: Global leader in event ticketing and management
**Users**: Millions of organizers and attendees worldwide

#### Pricing Model
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Free Events** | $0 for organizers |
| **Paid Events** | 3.7% + $1.79 per ticket + 2.9% payment processing |
| **Premium Plans** | Essentials, Professional, Premium (quote-based) |

#### Key Features
- ✅ Event creation and management
- ✅ Built-in payment processing
- ✅ Marketing tools and email campaigns
- ✅ Analytics dashboard
- ✅ Mobile app for organizers
- ✅ RSVP management
- ✅ Promotional tools (discount codes, early bird)

#### Strengths
- **Massive reach**: World's largest event marketplace
- **SEO power**: Events rank well on Google
- **Payment infrastructure**: Secure, reliable ticketing
- **Brand recognition**: Trusted by major venues
- **Integration ecosystem**: Works with many tools

#### Weaknesses
- ❌ **No dynamic pricing**: Fixed ticket prices only
- ❌ **No real-time notifications**: Email-based only
- ❌ **High fees**: Can be expensive for small organizers
- ❌ **Transaction-based pricing**: Penalizes successful events
- ❌ **No perishable inventory focus**: Treats all events the same
- ❌ **Limited discovery**: Browse/search only, no proactive matching
- ❌ **Generic platform**: One-size-fits-all approach

#### Target Market
- Large events (concerts, conferences)
- Professional event organizers
- Venues with fixed capacity
- Paid ticket events

---

### 2. Meetup (The Community Builder)

**Founded**: 2002 (acquired by WeWork, then separate)
**Market Position**: Community-focused event platform
**Users**: 50M+ members, 100K+ organizers

#### Pricing Model
| Tier | Price | Features |
|------|-------|----------|
| **Member** | Free | Join groups, attend events |
| **Organizer** | $16.49-$47/mo | Create groups, unlimited events |

#### Key Features
- ✅ Recurring meetups (weekly/monthly)
- ✅ Group-based structure
- ✅ Member management
- ✅ Discussion forums
- ✅ RSVP tracking
- ✅ Photo sharing
- ✅ Event sponsorship

#### Strengths
- **Community focus**: Built around interest groups
- **Recurring events**: Perfect for regular meetups
- **Member engagement**: Discussion boards, photos
- **Discoverability**: Members find groups by interest
- **Social aspect**: Builds ongoing relationships

#### Weaknesses
- ❌ **Expensive for organizers**: $200-$560/year
- ❌ **Group-centric**: Must create group first
- ❌ **No ticketing**: Basic event fees only (PayPal integration)
- ❌ **No dynamic pricing**: Fixed pricing only
- ❌ **No proactive notifications**: Email digests only
- ❌ **Limited to community events**: Not for commercial venues
- ❌ **No business tools**: No analytics, CRM, or marketing
- ❌ **Declining popularity**: Users migrating to alternatives

#### Target Market
- Hobby groups (hiking, board games, language exchange)
- Professional networking
- Support groups
- Community organizations
- Non-profits

---

### 3. Nextdoor (The Neighborhood Network)

**Founded**: 2011
**Market Position**: Private social network for neighborhoods
**Users**: 80M+ verified neighbors

#### Pricing Model
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Neighbors** | Free |
| **Businesses** | Free basic listing, paid advertising |
| **Events** | Free to create and share |

#### Key Features
- ✅ Location-based (hyperlocal)
- ✅ Verified neighbors (address confirmation)
- ✅ Community guidelines enforcement
- ✅ Local business integration
- ✅ Safety alerts
- ✅ Lost & found
- ✅ Local recommendations

#### Strengths
- **Hyperlocal**: Focused on immediate neighborhood
- **Trust**: Verified addresses create accountability
- **Free**: No cost to post events
- **Built-in audience**: Neighbors already on platform
- **Multi-purpose**: Not just events (alerts, classifieds)

#### Weaknesses
- ❌ **Limited to neighborhoods**: 1-2 mile radius
- ❌ **No ticketing**: No payment processing
- ❌ **No business tools**: No analytics or marketing
- ❌ **No dynamic pricing**: Simple announcement only
- ❌ **Limited discovery**: Only neighbors see events
- ❌ **No professional features**: For casual use only
- ❌ **Privacy concerns**: Some users uncomfortable with verification
- ❌ **Moderation issues**: Can be toxic, negative communities

#### Target Market
- Neighborhood block parties
- Garage sales
- Community meetings
- Lost pet alerts
- Local business promotions
- HOA announcements

**Market Position**: Built-in social feature

- **Completely Free**

- ❌ **No dynamic pricing**: Fixed price only
- ❌ **No professional features**: For casual use
- ❌ **Declining trust**: Users leaving platform
- ❌ **Ad dependency**: Organic reach decreasing

## 🔍 Competitive Gaps We Fill

### 1. Perishable Inventory Focus

**What it means**: Events where value drops to zero after the time passes

**Examples**:
- Cinema seats (empty after showtime)
- Fitness class spots (empty after class starts)
- Restaurant tables (empty after reservation time)
- Theater seats
- Comedy show tickets
- Yoga/Pilates classes
- Spa appointments

**Current Solutions**:
- Eventbrite: Generic ticketing, no optimization
- Meetup: Community focus, not commercial
- Others: No specific tools

**Our Advantage**:
- AI recommends optimal discount levels
- Demand forecasting before publishing
- Smart audience targeting for empty seats
- Last-minute deal notifications

### 2. Dynamic Pricing

**What it means**: Prices change based on demand, time, occupancy

**Eventbrite Approach**:
- Fixed early bird pricing
- Manual discount codes
- No automation

**Our Approach**:
- AI analyzes historical data
- Automatic price adjustments
- Optimal discount timing
- Revenue maximization

**Real-world example**:
```
Cinema Tuesday 7pm showing:
- Eventbrite: Fixed $12 ticket, hope people buy
- EventRadius: AI suggests $8 (40% off) at 5pm if <50% sold
  → Sends push notification to nearby movie fans
  → Fills seats that would have been empty
```

### 3. Proactive Notifications

**What it means**: Platform finds users, not the other way around

**Current Solutions**:
- All competitors: Users must search/browse
- Email digests (slow, batch)
- Algorithm feeds (unpredictable)

**Our Solution**:
- "Smart alerting": Predict when user is likely to engage
- Context-aware: Location + time + preferences + price sensitivity
- Push notifications: Instant, actionable

**Example**:
```
User context: Likes movies, free tonight, within 3 miles, price-sensitive
EventRadius AI: "40% off movie nearby tonight at 7:30pm"
Result: Conversion vs. user never seeing the event
```

### 4. SaaS vs. Transaction Pricing

**Competitor Model**:
- Eventbrite: 3.7% + $1.79 per ticket
- Penalizes successful events
- High fees for organizers

**Our Model**:
- Monthly subscription (Free/Starter/Pro/Enterprise)
- Predictable costs
- Scales with organizer needs, not ticket sales
- Free for casual users (3 events)

**Example Comparison**:
```
Organizer selling 100 tickets at $20:
- Eventbrite: 100 × ($20 × 3.7% + $1.79) = $255 in fees
- EventRadius Pro: $29/month flat rate
- Savings: $226/month
```
